III. Introduction
Semantic Sensing Wireless Networks Integrate Semantic Codec into Communication Systems: At the network layer, technologies such as beamforming and power allocation are utilized to suppress channel fading and interference; at the link layer, semantic resource allocation methods like adaptive semantic compression rates enhance experience quality. Unlike traditional experiences that primarily focus on the fidelity of bit-level signal transmission, semantic experience quality emphasizes the integrity and contextual relevance of the transmitted content. It can more accurately assess semantic transmission performance, which can be modeled as a function of semantic precision and semantic rate. Semantic precision measures the distance between the original information and the restored information, while semantic rate reflects the transmission rate of semantic information.
However, the joint optimization of cross-layer objectives in the network layer and link layer presents significant complexity: Traditional wireless networks contain a multitude of optimization parameters and variables, while semantic transmission introduces a series of high-dimensional optimization objectives, creating multi-dimensional optimization challenges. Therefore, how to effectively manage and coordinate these optimization objectives to enable network self-configuration and self-optimization to adapt to dynamic environments and requirements at both the network layer and link layer is an urgent issue to be addressed.
The core objective of the network layer is to effectively suppress inter-cell interference, laying a reliable foundation for semantic transmission at the link layer. Base stations need to dynamically adjust beam direction and transmit power based on real-time shared information. While enhancing the current link signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio, it should avoid causing interference to adjacent devices. In the future, the scale of the cell may further decrease, while the number of wireless devices will explode. The massive volume of traffic and signaling overhead will lead to a decline in transmission performance.
In the link layer, neural network-based semantic communication technology can efficiently compress large source data into compact semantic features, eliminating redundancy in high-dimensional data. It has significant advantages in data representation, compression, and transmission. Semantic features capture the intrinsic meaning of source data, often obtained from the intermediate outputs of neural networks, are abstract and subjective, and semantic compression is task-specific, relying on specific needs of intelligent applications. Due to a lack of unified standards, it also possesses robustness and flexibility.
Additionally, continuous optimization of the network layer faces numerous challenges: the construction of cache and synchronization mechanisms required for multi-base station collaboration is time-consuming, and it generates a significant amount of information interaction overhead. Frequent network reconfiguration also consumes substantial resources such as computational power and forwarding capacity. Therefore, network layer optimization is more suitable for large time scales. Conversely, the link layer's transmission content and user demand changes rapidly, requiring frequent adaptation at small time scales to enhance experience quality. Directly constructing the combined configuration of the network layer and link layer as a traditional single-layer optimization problem will face multiple issues such as high computational complexity, limited scalability, and the need for continuous reoptimization.
This paper proposes a distributed hierarchical deep reinforcement learning framework to maximize the overall wireless network experience quality performance from two time resolutions: coarse-grained dimension optimization for inter-cell interference at the network layer and finer-grained time scale optimization for real-time semantic resource allocation at the link layer. The service time is divided into continuous frames, each containing a fixed number of slots. At the larger time scale, beamforming and power allocation configurations are focused on maximizing the signal-to-interference-plus-noise ratio (SINR), while at the smaller time scale, experiences quality are enhanced by focusing on semantic resource allocation, improving both semantic rate and accuracy. In each frame, long-term beamforming and power allocation strategies are updated based on frame-level observation information, and short-term semantic resource allocation is optimized based on slot-level real-time continuous information.

5. Conclusion
This paper constructs a multi-cell, multi-user semantic perception wireless network, proposing a novel distributed joint beamforming, power allocation, and semantic resource allocation scheme that adopts multi-timescale control strategies. To address this joint optimization problem, this paper decouples it into two subproblems corresponding to infinite and finite Markov decision processes, designing a distributed hierarchical deep reinforcement learning architecture (DHDRL) with beamforming and power allocation as the primary controllers and semantic resource allocation as the controllers.
Numerical results show that the proposed distributed hierarchical deep reinforcement learning framework can efficiently solve complex multi-timescale decision multi-objective optimization problems, enabling the configuration of adaptable network layers and link layers parameters for semantic perception communication networks, achieving robust adaptation to dynamic network environments while meeting diverse semantic demands.
